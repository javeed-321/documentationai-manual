Resource Management helps you control how many rooms and extra services can be booked on your trips.

It is useful when you only have a limited number of real things to sell. That could be 10 Deluxe Rooms, 4 private transfers, or 12 sets of trekking gear.

Resource Management is an add-on. An add-on is a small extension that adds extra features to WP Travel Engine.

You can create a capacity rule that tells the system how many of something can be booked. For example, you might cap Ocean View rooms across all your coastal departures. Or you might limit kayak rentals on a single day tour.

You can apply a rule to every trip or only to selected trips. That means you stay in control without changing the rest of your site.

## Key features and benefits

| Feature                          | Benefit                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Room and service capacity limits | No more selling more rooms or extra services than you physically have.                                                              |
| One rule, many trips             | Apply the same capacity to every departure that shares a room type.                                                                 |
| Flexible capacity resets         | Decide whether a limit applies per departure or is shared across overlapping stays, depending on how you actually manage inventory. |
| Occupancy Calendar               | See rooms occupied and services booked for any date.                                                                                |
| Built-in Waitlist integration    | Release freed-up capacity to a specific waitlisted traveler and notify them directly.                                               |

These tools are there to help you avoid overbooking. Next, make sure the pieces you need are already in place.

## What you'll need before you start

A plugin is a piece of software that adds features to WordPress. Resource Management works alongside WP Travel Engine and the related add-ons below.

- You have WordPress 6.5 or higher installed on your site.
- You have WP Travel Engine installed and activated on your WordPress site.
- You have updated WP Travel Engine to its latest version (Version 6.8.5 or above).
- You have installed and activated the Resource Management add-on, with a valid license key.
- You have installed and activated at least one of the following:
  - Accommodation (Version 1.0.9 or higher)
  - Extra Services (Version 2.2.2 or higher)

Once these are ready, you can confirm the add-on is showing in your dashboard.

## Creating a capacity rule

A capacity rule tells Resource Management what to limit and where that limit should apply.

For example, you might create one rule for Deluxe Rooms across all your summer departures. Or you might create another rule for camera rentals on one photography trip.

From your WordPress dashboard, go to **WP Travel Engine > Resource Management** and click **Add Rule** to open the Configure New Capacity Rule panel.

### Rule basics

These settings decide what the rule covers before you set the actual numbers.

- **Rule Name**: Give the rule a name you will recognize later. A name like "Deluxe Room, Summer Season" works well. Travelers do not see this name. It is only for you on the rules list.
- **Applies To**: Choose **All Trips** if the same limit should apply everywhere. Choose **Specific Trips** if you only want the rule on selected trips. When you choose Specific Trips, a drop-down appears so you can search for and select the trips you want.
- **Limit to Date Ranges**: Turn this on if the rule should only work during a certain period. Then set the **Start Date** and **End Date**. Only trips departing between those dates will use the rule. This is helpful for seasonal limits, such as a peak-season room cap or winter gear rental limit. Leave it off if you want the rule to stay active all the time.
- **Track Capacity For**: Choose Accommodation, Extra Services, or both. Each choice opens its own settings area below. That means one rule can control rooms and services together if you want.

Once the basics are set, you can choose how room bookings or service bookings should reduce availability.

### Accommodation restriction

If you sell rooms on your trips, this section controls how those room limits are counted.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1786956528152-i9ra9av40qi-resource-management-in-configure-new-capacity-rule-accommodation.resource-management-in-configure-new-capacity-rule-accommodation?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="764" height="904" alt="" />

**How should bookings reduce capacity?** decides how the system counts your room limit when trips have different departure dates.

**On the departure date only**

Each departure keeps its own separate room count. A booking on one departure does not reduce room capacity on another departure.
**Example:** Room A is capped at 10 units. A 3-day trip departs on June 1st and June 2nd. Booking 5 units of Room A on June 1st leaves June 1st with 5 units left, but June 2nd still has all 10, since each departure has its own capacity.

**Across the entire trip duration**

The room stays are counted as booked for the full stay. If another departure overlaps those same dates, it uses the same shared room pool.
**Example:** Room A is capped at 10 units. A trip departs on June 1st for a 5-night stay, and another trip departs on June 3rd for a 4-night stay. If the June 1st trip books 6 units of Room A, those rooms stay held for its whole stay, through June 6th. Since the June 3rd trip's dates fall inside that same window, only 4 units of Room A are left for it to book.

This choice matters most when the same real room inventory is shared between overlapping trips.

**Room Types & Upgrades**: Select the rooms and upgrades this rule should cover. After you select them, each one appears in a table below with an **Exclusive Capacity** box. Enter how many of that room can be booked.

For example, if you only have 10 Deluxe Rooms and 4 Ocean View upgrades, enter those numbers here. That way the booking system follows the real stock you have available.

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
  Leave a room's capacity box empty if you only want to track it in the Occupancy Calendar and do not want to limit bookings yet.
</Callout>

Once your room settings are in place, you can set limits for extra services in the same rule if needed.

### Extra Services restriction

If you offer bookable add-ons like water, private transfers, cameras, or gear rental, this section lets you cap those too.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1786956949795-fj3i5kmlew7-resource-management-in-configure-new-capacity-rule-extra-services.resource-management-in-configure-new-capacity-rule-extra-services?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="720" height="772" alt="" />

**How should bookings reduce capacity?** Works the same way as it does for rooms.

- **On the departure date only** gives each departure its own separate service slots. **Example**: Room A is capped at 10 units. A 3-day trip departs on June 1st and June 2nd. Booking 5 units of Room A on June 1st leaves June 1st with 5 units left, but June 2nd still has all 10, since each departure has its own capacity.
- **Across the entire trip duration,** shares those slots across departures whose dates overlap.&#x20;
  **Example**: Room A is capped at 10 units. A trip departs on June 1st for a 5-night stay, and another trip departs on June 3rd for a 4-night stay. If the June 1st trip books 6 units of Room A, those rooms stay held for its whole stay, through June 6th. Since the June 3rd trip’s dates fall inside that same window, only 4 units of Room A are left for it to book.

This is helpful when a service is tied to the full trip. A camping kit, for example, may be unavailable for the whole trek once booked.

**Services**: Click this field and choose the extra services this rule should apply to, such as Water or Camera. You can select more than one. Once selected, each service appears in the table below with a **Total Slots** box. Enter the maximum number of bookings that service can receive.

For example, if you only have 8 cameras available for a photography day tour, enter 8 in the service limit. Once all 8 are booked, no one else can book that service.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  A service that runs out on one date won't block a different date unless "Across the entire trip duration" is selected.
</Callout>

Once everything is set, click **Add Rule** and click **Save Changes.**

That is the main setup done. Next, you can choose what travelers will see on the trip page.

## Controlling what travelers see

Resource Management stores the rules and capacity numbers behind the scenes.

What travelers see on the trip page is controlled in the settings for Accommodation and Extra Services. This gives you a separate place to decide whether people should see low-stock notices, sold-out labels, or waitlist options.

### Capacity Management in Accommodation

This section controls how room availability appears to travelers during booking.

From your WP dashboard, go to **WP Travel Engine > Settings > Extensions > Accommodation** and select the **Capacity Management** tab.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1786957074779-gi669i9yb2-resource-management-capacity-management-accommodation.resource-management-capacity-management-accommodation?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="643" alt="" />

- **Show Availability Count**: Turn this on if you want travelers to see an "X Left" message next to a room when stock is getting low.
- **Show Sold-out Room**: Turn this on if you want a full room to stay visible on the trip page with a "Sold out" label instead of disappearing.
- **Allow Waitlist When a Room is Fully Booked**: Turn this on if you want travelers to join a waitlist when a room has sold out.

<Callout kind="alert" collapsed="false">
  The Waitlist add-on must be installed, activated, and updated to version 1.0.4 or later to use this feature.
</Callout>

You can release rooms to your waitlisters later from the Upcoming Tours dashboard. That screen shows all waitlisters for a specific departure alongside your trip waitlisters. Click **Save Settings** when you are done.

Once room display settings are set, you can do the same for extra services.

### Capacity Management in Extra Services

This works the same way as Accommodation. It controls what travelers see for bookable services like gear rental or private transfers.

From your WordPress dashboard, go to **WP Travel Engine > Settings > Extensions > Extra Services**.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1786957134521-edhwa4u90pu-resource-management-capacity-management-extra-services.resource-management-capacity-management-extra-services?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="696" alt="" />

Under your existing settings, you will find the same **Show Availability Count** and **Show Sold-out Service** toggles. They do the same job here, but for extra services instead of rooms.

With those settings in place, you can see what the booking experience looks like from the traveler's side.

## Viewing available and booked resources in the Occupancy Calendar

The Occupancy Calendar gives you a date-by-date view of what is already booked.

This is helpful when you want to check room use across several departures or see how many add-on services are already taken on a busy day.

From your WordPress dashboard, go to **WP Travel Engine > Resource Management > Occupancy Calendar**.

Each day shows how many rooms are occupied and how many services are booked, based on every active rule. Click a day to open its breakdown. The breakdown shows all the rooms and extra services booked that day, and how many are booked per trip.

## Use cases

These examples show how Resource Management works in real tour businesses. If you are not sure how to set up your own rules yet, start here.

### Capping a popular room type across the season

**Situation:** You run multi-day packages and sell a Deluxe Room on several departures. You only have 10 real Deluxe Rooms available across the season. Without a shared limit, overlapping departures could sell more rooms than you actually have.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the rule" title-type="p">
    This starts the room limit setup.

    Go to **WP Travel Engine Dashboard > Resource Management** and click **Add Rule**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Name and scope it" title-type="p">
    This helps you recognize the rule later and apply it widely.

    Name it "Deluxe Room, Season Cap" and set **Applies To** to **All Trips**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Track Accommodation capacity" title-type="p">
    This tells the system you are limiting rooms, not services.

    Under **Track Capacity For**, tick **Accommodation**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the room capacity" title-type="p">
    This is where you enter the real number you can sell.

    Select the Deluxe Room and set its capacity to 10.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Share the pool across overlapping stays" title-type="p">
    This makes overlapping departures draw from the same room stock.

    Choose "Across the entire trip duration" so overlapping stays share the same pool.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save the rule" title-type="p">
    This turns the rule into an active limit.

    Click **Save Changes**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Benefit:** Every departure checks against the same 10-room limit. No trip can oversell the room, even if several departures list it.

### Reserving a freed-up seat for a specific waitlisted traveler

**Situation:** Your Ocean View room sold out on an upcoming departure. Then one traveler canceled. You want a returning customer on the waitlist to get first access before anyone else sees that space.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the trip's waitlist details" title-type="p">
    This is where you manage who is waiting for that departure.

    Go to **WP Travel Engine Dashboard > Upcoming Tours** and open the trip's **View Details**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Release a seat" title-type="p">
    This sets aside room space for a waitlisted traveler.

    Under **Room Capacity Management**, release 1 seat for Ocean View.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Find the waitlisted customer" title-type="p">
    This is where you choose who should receive access.

    Find the customer's waitlist entry and click **Send Email**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Send the booking link" title-type="p">
    This gives that traveler a private way to book the opened space.

    Send the private booking link.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Benefit:** Only that traveler can book the newly opened room, and their status updates once the email goes out, so you can see they have been contacted.

These are two common setups. If something is not behaving the way you expect, check the fixes below.

## The Resource Management menu isn't showing up in my dashboard

Start by checking that the add-on is installed, activated, and has a valid license key. The menu appears under **WP Travel Engine** in the left sidebar of your WordPress dashboard.

If it still does not show, go to **Plugins**, deactivate Resource Management, and activate it again. Then refresh your dashboard and check the menu one more time.

## My capacity rule isn't limiting bookings on the trip page

First, open the rule and make sure it is set to the correct trips, date range, and resource type. A rule set to **Specific Trips** will not affect other trips.

Then check that you entered a number in the room **Exclusive Capacity** field or the service **Total Slots** field. If the field is left empty, the item can still appear in the Occupancy Calendar without being limited.

## Rooms are showing as sold out when they shouldn't be

Check how the rule counts capacity. If you selected **Across the entire trip duration**, overlapping departures share the same room stock.

That means one booking can reduce availability on another departure if the stay dates overlap. If you want each departure to have its own separate room count, switch the rule to **On the departure date only**.

## The Occupancy Calendar is empty or not showing my bookings

The Occupancy Calendar only shows rooms and services that have actually been booked. It does not show items that only have a rule set up without any real bookings yet.

If you expected bookings to appear, check that the trips have confirmed bookings for the rooms or services covered by your active rules.

## I released a seat for a waitlisted traveler but it's not showing on the calendar

That is expected behavior. Releasing capacity for a waitlisted traveler does not count as a booking yet.

The calendar updates only after that traveler completes their booking using the link you sent them.

## Travelers can still book more rooms than my capacity limit allows

Check that the correct rooms or services were selected inside the rule. Then confirm you clicked **Add Rule** and **Save Changes** after entering the capacity numbers.

Also make sure the related add-on is active. Room limits require Accommodation. Service limits require Extra Services. If the setup is right and overbooking still happens, review whether another rule, trip selection, or date range is allowing bookings outside the limit.

## Frequently asked questions

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="Does Resource Management replace the trip seat limits WP Travel Engine already has?" default-open="false">
    No. Resource Management adds a separate limit for rooms and extra services. Your normal trip-level seat limits still work the same way.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What happens if I don't set a capacity for a room or service?" default-open="false">
    The room or service can still be tracked in the Occupancy Calendar without an enforced booking limit. This is useful when you want visibility first and want to decide on limits later.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can one rule cover rooms and services from different trips?" default-open="false">
    Yes. You can do that when **Applies To** is set to **All Trips**, or when you add the right trips under **Specific Trips**.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Does releasing capacity for a waitlisted traveler show on the Occupancy Calendar right away?" default-open="false">
    No. Releasing capacity only gives that waitlisted traveler a booking link. The Occupancy Calendar updates only after they finish the booking.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What happens if I deactivate a rule?" default-open="false">
    The rule stops enforcing its capacity. Any rooms or services covered by that rule return to their normal availability without that extra limit.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Does this work with Activity Tour Booking's time slots?" default-open="false">
    Yes. The rules work the same way. The only difference is that released capacity for a waitlisted traveler applies only to the specific time slot they were waitlisted for, not the whole day.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
