Your Single Trip page is the main page travelers see before they book. It is where they look at photos, check prices, read trip details, and decide whether to enquire or reserve a spot.

A few small settings here can change how clear and trustworthy that page feels. If you are setting this up for the first time, take it one section at a time.

## Accessing Single Trip settings

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Before you start, log in to your WordPress dashboard. That is the main admin area of your website, with the menu on the left sidebar.
</Callout>

Use these steps to open the Single Trip settings area.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your WordPress dashboard" icon="home" title-type="p">
    If you are not logged in yet, sign in to your WordPress Admin Dashboard.

    You should now see the main admin menu on the left side of the screen.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Go to WP Travel Engine settings" icon="settings" title-type="p">
    In the left sidebar, click **WP Travel Engine**.

    Then click **Settings**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open the Single Trip settings" icon="folder" title-type="p">
    At the top of the settings page, click **Display**.

    Then open **Single Trip**.

    You are now in the place where you can control how each trip page looks and works.

    <Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787471736699-ocxjgzji8qd-single-trip-setting-banner-layout.single-trip-setting-banner-layout?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="419" alt="" />
  </Step>
</Steps>

Once you are here, you can start with the top of the page and work your way down.

## Banner layouts

The banner is the first thing travelers notice on your trip page. A good layout helps your trip look polished right away.

If you run photo-heavy trips like treks, safaris, or multi-day adventure packages, this section can make a big difference. You can choose from 7 banner layouts based on how you want your images to appear.

### Banner Layout 1

This layout shows a full-width image slider with navigation arrows.

Visitors can move through your gallery images using the slider or the arrows. They can also click the **Gallery** button in the bottom-right corner to open a Lightbox modal. A Lightbox modal is a pop-up image viewer that shows the full-sized photos without sending the visitor to a new page.

Recommended image size: 1920 × 800 pixels.

This works well if you want travelers to get a strong first impression from large destination photos.

## Image and gallery settings

These settings control which images travelers see and how those images behave. If photos help you sell your trips, this section is worth a quick check.

**Show Trip Featured Image** - Displays the featured image on the trip detail page.

**Show Featured Image in Image Gallery** - Includes the featured image in the trip's image gallery, both on the single trip page and on the trip card.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787472233409-xj4y0yvofg-single-trip-setting-feature-image.single-trip-setting-feature-image?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="768" height="342" alt="" />

- **Image Gallery Autoplay** - Automatically cycles through images in the trip's image gallery. Disabled by default.&#x20;
  <Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787472275136-344r53ufsbc-single-trip-setting-image-autoplay.single-trip-setting-image-autoplay?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="768" height="63" alt="" />

For example, if you only have three strong photos for a day hike, you may want to keep the featured image in the gallery so the page feels fuller. If you already use that image at the top, you may prefer to leave it out of the gallery to avoid repeating it.

With your images in place, you can move on to how your trip details are shown.

## Show Booking Form

The booking form is the part of the page where travelers start the reservation process. If you want people to book directly from the trip page, this needs to be visible.

When enabled, the Booking Form appears on the trip detail page. When disabled, the Booking Form is hidden.

If your trip page has a lot of content, travelers can lose track of the tabs as they scroll. This option helps keep navigation usable.

When enabled, the trip content tabs become scrollable, keeping all content accessible on longer trip detail pages.

## Show warning message in booking modal

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787474419680-4izlv1zgj87-single-trip-setting-warning-message-booking-model.single-trip-setting-warning-message-booking-model?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="165" alt="" />

A booking modal is the pop-up window travelers see when they begin booking. This message helps you set expectations before they confirm anything.

Add a warning message shown inside the booking modal, informing travelers about the minimum participant requirement. Include the placeholder `{min_pax}` in the message to automatically display the required minimum number of participants.

For example, you could write: `This trip runs with a minimum of {min_pax} travelers.`

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787474450976-5ihqqe8svej-single-trip-setting-warning-message-front-end.single-trip-setting-warning-message-front-end?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="645" alt="" />

That way, if you run a small-group trek that only departs with four people, the traveler sees that clearly before booking.

## Pricing Widget Layouts (for smaller devices)

Many travelers check trips on their phones first. This section helps your pricing widget stay clear on smaller screens.

A widget is a built-in content box that shows something important on the page. In this case, it is the pricing area.

Navigate to **WP Travel Engine > Settings > Display > Single Trip > Pricing Widget** to choose your preferred layout:

- **Default** - A mobile-friendly layout with a toggle-style button that lets visitors show or hide prices. Ideal for a clean, user-friendly design.

## Show WhatsApp

## Enquiry Message

This message helps point travelers toward the next action. It is useful when someone is interested but not ready to book.

Enter the enquiry message displayed in the trip pricing widget. Include `[[Your Link Label]]` in the message to show a clickable link directing users to the enquiry form.

For example, you might write something like: Need help choosing dates? `[[Send us an enquiry]]`

That gives travelers a clear path if they have questions about private departures, group rates, or add-ons like gear rental.

Now you can decide how the enquiry form itself should work.

## Enquiry Form

The enquiry form gives travelers a way to contact you from the trip page. This is often useful for custom tours, private trips, or travelers who need more details before booking.

Configure the enquiry form via **WP Travel Engine > Settings > Display > Single Trip > Enquiry Form**.

### Show Enquiry Form

Turn this on if you want an enquiry form to appear on single trip posts. This helps people contact you without leaving the trip page.

Enable this toggle to display the enquiry form on a single trip post.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787475355920-gamra0vhvao-single-trip-setting-show-enquiry-form.single-trip-setting-show-enquiry-form?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="332" alt="" />

If you do a lot of tailor-made tours, this is often worth keeping on.

### Enquiry Info

This setting controls where the enquiry link sends the traveler. That matters if you want to keep them on the same page or send them somewhere else.

Enable this option to display an enquiry form on the single trip page. By default, this links to the enquiry section of the page. Enabling it reveals additional settings:

- **Default** - The enquiry form links to the enquiry section of the page, where travelers submit questions or requests.

For example, you might use **Default** for normal trip questions. You might choose **Custom** if you send luxury trip enquiries to a separate consultation page.

### Custom Enquiry Form

A custom enquiry form lets you use a form built with another WordPress form plugin. A plugin is an add-on tool that gives your site extra features.

Enable this option to use a custom enquiry form. Make sure the custom form includes a notification email to receive submissions, then add the shortcode for your custom form. A shortcode is a small piece of text that tells WordPress where to display something, such as a contact form. WP Travel Engine is compatible with popular form plugins such as Gravity Forms, Ninja Forms, and WPForms.

### GDPR Message

GDPR is a privacy rule that explains how businesses should collect and handle personal information. This message helps reassure travelers when they submit their details.

Enter the GDPR message shown on the trip enquiry form.

This is a good place to let people know how you will use their information, such as replying to their trip question or sending a quote.

## Show Related Trips

Related trips help travelers keep browsing. They are especially useful when someone likes one trip but wants a different duration, activity, or destination.

Displays related trips on the single trip page. Once enabled, you get these configuration options:

- **Related Section Title** - A custom title for the related trips section.
- **No. of Related Trips to Display** - The number of related trips to show.
- **Enable Related Trips By** - Which taxonomies (Destinations, Activities, Trip Types, Difficulty, Tags) to use for filtering related trips.

For example, if someone lands on a 7-day Annapurna trek, you might want to show other Nepal treks, similar difficulty trips, or trips in the same activity type.
