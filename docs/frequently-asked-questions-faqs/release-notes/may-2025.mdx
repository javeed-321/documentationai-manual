## Scrut Innovations May 2025: Risk Discovery, Policy Variables, and Trust Portal Branding

**Meta description:** Build a risk discovery library, set policy variables, brand your trust portal, and track Cyber Essentials v3.2 or Botswana DPA controls in Scrut Automation.

May 2025 expands how compliance teams discover risks, keep policies consistent, present a branded trust experience, and cover more frameworks and integrations inside Scrut Automation.

## Brand your trust portal for every visitor

Most security and compliance teams still share a trust portal that looks generic. Prospects notice when logos, colors, and fonts do not match the rest of the company site, and that mismatch slows confidence during security reviews.

Scrut Automation's Trust Vault Branding tab now lets you set organization name, page title, logo, favicon, brand color, and typeface for the portal. Brand color can be entered as RGB, hex, or a picker value, and it flows through buttons, icons, and hyperlinks, with a path back to defaults when you need to reset. External visitors see a portal that matches your brand system instead of a one-size-fits-all shell. Explore more on Scrut's [Trust Center capability page](https://www.scrut.io/platform/trust-center).

## Add curated risks to your register faster

Risk owners often rebuild common risk catalogs from scratch. Spreadsheets and tribal knowledge leave gaps, and category coverage depends on whoever last updated the register.

Scrut Automation's Risk Discovery tab in Risk Management is a categorized library of potential risks you can add to the register with one action. Filters separate risks already added from those still available, and category filters help you focus the list before you commit items. Teams populate a usable register faster without inventing every entry from a blank page.

## Standardize shared details across policy templates

Policy authors repeat organization name, legal entities, product name, and scope across dozens of templates. When one detail changes, teams chase every document by hand and still miss stale values.

Scrut Automation's Policy Variables feature collects those answers in one form and maps them into policies after Submit, moving previously not-uploaded policies into draft with variables applied. In the policy editor, you can reuse a variable elsewhere in the same policy and remove a variable when it no longer applies, with an option to revert to the original template if custom content was cleared. Consistency improves because the shared values live in one place instead of scattered copy-paste.

## Operationalize Botswana Data Protection Act controls

Organizations that process personal data tied to Botswana face a refreshed privacy statute while still running programs built for other jurisdictions. Mapping obligations across local and global frameworks by hand delays readiness reviews.

Scrut Automation now includes the Botswana Data Protection Act (2024) framework, aligned to Act No. 18 of 2024, which commenced on 14 January 2025 and replaces the earlier 2018 Act. Teams can view pre-mapped controls, reuse cross-framework mappings, assign owners and deadlines, and export reports from the Frameworks module. Privacy leads get a single control set for Botswana obligations instead of maintaining a side spreadsheet.

## Track Cyber Essentials v3.2 baseline controls

UK-facing teams preparing Cyber Essentials certification need a clear control list for cloud services, multi-factor authentication (MFA), devices, and patch expectations. Version drift between internal checklists and the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) baseline creates audit friction.

Scrut Automation supports Cyber Essentials v3.2 in Frameworks with pre-mapped controls, owner assignment, status tracking, and readiness exports, plus cross-framework mappings to programs such as ISO 27001. Security leads can work from one structured baseline instead of reconciling a separate NCSC checklist outside the GRC system.

## Structure GLBA Safeguards and Privacy controls

Financial institutions and fintechs that handle nonpublic personal information (NPI) still assemble Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) evidence from scattered policies and tickets. Gaps between the Safeguards Rule and Privacy Rule obligations surface late in audits.

Scrut Automation ships an out-of-the-box GLBA framework with pre-mapped controls so teams can centralize evidence, assign ownership, and spot Safeguards and Privacy practice gaps in one place. Compliance owners get a structured path to demonstrate federal financial privacy and security expectations without rebuilding the control inventory each cycle.

## Sync clearer compliance work into Jira

Engineering queues bury compliance tasks when titles are cryptic, due dates are missing, and descriptions lack remediation context. Reviewers spend more time decoding tickets than closing them.

Scrut Automation's enhanced Jira integration creates tickets titled `[Scrut - <Module>] <Task Title>`, offers due-date choices of 1, 3, 7, 15, or 30 days that sync to Jira's native due date field, and can Auto Fill a Markdown description with origin, requirements, users, and remediation guidance. Cross-functional teams see compliance work with the same clarity as other engineering tickets. Learn more about audit and remediation workflows on Scrut's [Audit Center page](https://www.scrut.io/platform/audit-center).

## Connect Sophos Central for device evidence

Endpoint fleets managed in Sophos Central often stay disconnected from the compliance system of record. Device population, encryption status, and ownership still get exported manually before access reviews and tests.

Scrut Automation's Sophos Central Mobile Device Manager (MDM) integration pulls employee devices and computer assets into asset management and Access Review, and powers automated tests for hard-disk encryption, MDM or agent monitoring, assigned asset owners, and tracking of assets that hold critical data. IT and compliance teams reuse Sophos inventory as living evidence instead of refreshing spreadsheets for every review.

## Verify required software on employee devices

Auditors ask whether antivirus, data loss prevention (DLP), or other mandated agents are installed, and people teams still chase screenshots. Predetermined software categories go stale as the real inventory changes.

Scrut Automation's People checklist field Install Required Software lets admins select mandated software by operating system from MDM inventory, then auto-passes or fails the checklist based on whether those packages appear on the employee's devices (supported MDMs such as Scrut Agent). Endpoint software expectations become a checklist result, not a manual chase.

## Capture multi-select answers in vendor questionnaires

Vendor questionnaires force single answers on questions that need several, such as which personal data types a vendor processes. Teams invent free-text workarounds and lose clean scoring.

Scrut Automation now supports multi-select objective questions in vendor questionnaire templates, imports, and intake forms, with checkboxes in the UI and risk scoring set to the maximum score among selected options. Vendor risk analysts collect complete answers without breaking the objective question model. See Scrut's [Vendor Risk Management page](https://www.scrut.io/platform/vendor-risk-management) for related assessment workflows.

## Integrations and collaboration alerts

Microsoft Teams can receive personal and employee task reminders plus alerts for Trust Center access, security reports, test status changes, Monitor health, integration failures, and vendor events after you connect the account and configure Settings → Notifications.

Access Review coverage expands to Calendly, Figma, and Apollo.io so more SaaS tools can enter user access campaigns.

## People and access review operations

Employee CSV import accepts an optional Group column, with either auto-create-and-assign behavior or assignment of everyone to one existing group during import.

Access reviews in Active, Overdue, or Completed states can be archived (Drafts cannot). Active and Overdue archives require a reason, archives are not restorable, and the audit log records user, timestamp, review name, and reason, with read-only access under an Archived tab.

Access Review listing and detail screens also receive tooltip, filter icon, and draft-state delete affordance polish.

## Controls, policy editor, and platform UX

Control descriptions now appear beside control names across Frameworks, Risk Management, Audit Center, Tests, Policies, Evidence Tasks, and Corrective Action tables and modals, and users can search controls by description keywords.

The policy editor locks a policy while another user is editing, shows an "Another user is editing" modal to others, releases the lock after idle or offline timeout, and offers Save Now when auto-save fails.

Admins can manage allowed login methods under Settings → Administration → Login Methods, including email/password, SSO providers such as Okta or Azure AD, and social logins, while keeping at least one method active.

Command+K or Ctrl+K navigation and content search cover tests, people, risk, policy names and document text, evidence names, and vault document text for supported file types, with phased customer rollout.

Platform-wide design fixes align edit-modal save behavior, dashboard card states, and integration card interactions.

## Vendor questionnaire polish

Vendor questions preserve line breaks and bullets instead of collapsing into one line, and the question character limit increases from 500 to 1000 characters.

## Continuous monitoring and automated tests

New automated tests expand coverage across Scrut-native checks and integrations such as GitHub, Microsoft Entra, Notion, Jira, Bitbucket, Cloudflare, Confluence, MongoDB Atlas, Linear, JumpCloud, DigitalOcean, Datadog, and GitLab, including deprovisioning, MFA, encryption, backup, and related control tests.

## Questionnaire automation polish

Proof of concept (POC) accounts are limited to three questionnaires with 20 questions each; a fourth upload is blocked, and questionnaires over 20 questions ingest only the first 20 with a warning, including via the Chrome extension.

When reviewers open an automated answer source for doc or pdf files, the exact source text is highlighted.

## Framework mapping maintenance

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Cyber Security Framework (CSF) mapping is streamlined: requirements move from 138 to 137, controls from 133 to 108, evidence items from 161 to 82, and policies from 41 to 21, reducing duplicate work while keeping coverage.
