## Offer travelers more flexible ways to pay

Installment Payments lets you collect trip payments in scheduled parts instead of asking for the full amount upfront. That can help more travelers say yes to higher-value trips.

Say you run a 10-day trek that costs $2,000. With a 4-installment plan, your traveler can pay $500 at a time instead of paying the full amount in one go. That can feel more manageable for them and help you secure the booking sooner.

An add-on is a plugin that adds a feature to your WordPress site. Stripe is an online payment processor that handles charging credit cards. With this add-on, Stripe handles the scheduled charges for you, so you do not need to chase each payment by hand.

Once you have it set up, travelers can see their payment plan at checkout and pay over time. Next, here is what the add-on gives you.

## Key features

- **Global and trip-specific settings** - Set installment plans for your whole site and choose whether individual trips should use them.
- **Multiple installment plans** - Offer different payment options, such as 10, 6, or 4 installments.
- **Optional installment surcharge** - Add an extra percentage when travelers choose to pay in installments.
- **Automatic payment scheduling** - The system calculates payment dates and amounts for you.
- **Stripe-powered payments** - Stripe processes scheduled installment payments automatically and securely.

These features give you control without adding extra admin work. Now you can see what that looks like in day-to-day use.

## What you'll need before you start

A few things need to be in place before installment payments can work properly.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  - You have WP Travel Engine installed and activated on your WordPress site. You need Version 6.7.0 or later.
  - You have installed and activated the [Stripe Payment Gateway add-on](/article/stripe-payment-gateway/) to its latest version.
  - You have installed and activated the Installment Payments add-on.
  - You have configured your Stripe webhook. A webhook is a way for Stripe to automatically notify your site when a payment happens. See [how to set up your webhook](/article/stripe-payment-gateway/).
</Callout>

<Callout kind="alert" collapsed="false">
  Make sure you have added these two events in Stripe: `invoice.payment_succeeded` and `invoice.payment_failed`, so that the system can track successful and failed payments.
</Callout>

Once these pieces are ready, you can check that the add-on is installed correctly.

## Setting up global settings

Global settings are the default rules for all your trips. You will find them in the left sidebar of your WordPress dashboard under **WP Travel Engine** → **Settings** → **Extensions** → **Installment Payment**

### Show Installment Payments

Turn **Show Installment Payments** on if you want this feature available across your site. If this is off, travelers will not see installment options at checkout.

This is the main switch for the feature. Once it is on, you can decide how the payment plans should work.

### Final Installment Due

This setting decides how many days before the trip starts the last payment must be completed. That helps you avoid carrying unpaid balances too close to departure.

For example, if you set this to 20 days and the trip starts on June 30, the final installment will be due by June 10.

That gives you a clear payment deadline. Next, you can build the plans travelers will choose from.

### Add New Plan

Each plan tells the system how to split the total trip cost.

#### Number of Installments

Choose how many payments the traveler will make. Common options are 4, 6, or 10 installments.

For example, if you sell a multi-day package for $1,200 and offer 4 installments, the traveler will pay the total across 4 scheduled payments. This helps you match the plan to the type of trip you sell.

#### Installment Cut-Off Days

Set the minimum number of days before the trip start date that a traveler must book to use installments. The minimum allowed value is 30 days.

This protects you from offering installment plans when there is not enough time left to complete them. For example, if the trip starts on June 30 and your cut-off is 20 days, travelers booking on or after June 10 will no longer see the installment option.

#### Increment Percentage

Use **Increment Percentage** if you want to add a surcharge for travelers who choose installments. Leave it blank if you do not want to charge anything extra.

For example, if your day tour package costs $800 and you add a 5% increment, the installment option will include that extra amount. This can help cover payment risk or admin costs.

Once your plans are in place, you can decide how the extra fee should appear to travelers.

### Installment Surcharge Label

Enter the name you want travelers to see for the surcharge at checkout. For example, you might use "Installment Fee."

A clear label helps avoid confusion during checkout. The last setting decides where that fee sits in the tax calculation.

### Installment Surcharge Position

Choose whether the surcharge is applied **Before Tax** or **After Tax**.

- **Before Tax** - Tax is calculated on the trip cost plus the installment surcharge.
- **After Tax** - Tax is calculated only on the trip cost, not on the installment surcharge.

This affects the final amount your traveler pays. Once your global settings are done, you can still control installments on specific trips.

## Setting up installment payments for individual trips

Sometimes you want one trip to follow your main rules and another trip to stay excluded. You can control that inside each trip.

In the left sidebar of your WordPress dashboard, open the trip and go to **WP Travel Engine** → **Trip Settings** → **Date and Price**. Scroll down to the **Installment Payments** setting.

You will see these options:

- **Use Global** - The trip uses the installment settings you created in the main Installment Payments area.
- **Disabled** - The trip does not offer installments, even if installments are turned on globally.

For example, you might use **Use Global** for a 7-day cultural tour that has enough lead time for scheduled payments. You might choose **Disabled** for a last-minute seasonal departure that starts too soon.

That gives you room to be flexible trip by trip. Next, let us look at the emails that keep everyone informed.

## Setting up retry policies in Stripe

Retry policies tell Stripe what to do when a scheduled card payment does not go through. This matters because failed payments happen for normal reasons, like low funds, expired cards, or bank declines.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Log in to Stripe" title-type="p">
    Open your **Stripe Dashboard** and sign in to your account.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Find Revenue recovery" title-type="p">
    Use the search bar at the top and search for **Revenue recovery**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open the Retries tab" title-type="p">
    Click the **Retries** tab to choose how Stripe should handle failed installment payments.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Now you can choose the retry style that fits how you want to run your business.

### Smart Retries

Choose this if you want Stripe to decide the best time to try the charge again. Stripe looks at payment patterns and chooses moments when the card is more likely to go through.

You can still control the limits. For example, you might allow Stripe to retry up to 8 times within 1 week.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/image-5.png" width="1024" height="730" alt="Smart Retry policy settings in Stripe" />

### Custom Schedule

Choose this if you want to decide exactly when retries happen. You can set the number of days between each attempt.

Stripe may suggest a schedule like 3, 5, and 7 days after the previous attempt. You can change those days to suit your booking flow. You can set a maximum of 3 retry attempts.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/image-6-1024x814.png" width="1024" height="814" alt="Custom retry policy schedule settings in Stripe" />

Once you save this in Stripe, it runs automatically in the background. The next setting decides what happens if all retries still fail.

### Subscription Status

This setting controls the result after Stripe has finished all retry attempts.

\< Image src="[https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1786852485551-zme2laxv2u-installment-payment-subscription-status.installment-payment-subscription-status?fm=auto\&auto=compress%2Cformat](https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1786852485551-zme2laxv2u-installment-payment-subscription-status.installment-payment-subscription-status?fm=auto\&auto=compress%2Cformat)" width="768" height="539" alt="" />

| Option                                                        | What it does                                                                                                                                              | Can it be resumed? |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Cancel the subscription (strict)                              | The installment plan is permanently canceled. The traveler would need to book again, or you would need to create a new manual order.                      | No                 |
| Mark the subscription as unpaid (recommended for flexibility) | The installment plan stays active, but the status changes to "Unpaid." Stripe stops retrying the card automatically, but the subscription is not deleted. | Yes                |
| Leave the subscription overdue                                | The installment plan stays active and remains in a "Past Due" state. This means the payment is late, but the subscription is still there.                 | Yes                |

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
  If you want the option to sort out a failed payment later, choose "Mark the subscription as unpaid." If you want the booking to cancel automatically after retries fail, choose "Cancel the subscription."
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

### Syncing mismatched payment status

Sometimes Stripe shows a payment as successful, but your WP Travel Engine booking still shows it as **Pending**. That usually means the payment happened, but your site did not update the status properly.

This can happen for two common reasons:

- The Installment Payments add-on was deactivated when the scheduled payment happened.
- A temporary sync gap caused one payment to update and another one to stay behind. For example, Payment 4 may show as completed while Payment 3 still shows as pending.

This fix checks Stripe and updates the booking with the real payment status.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the booking" title-type="p">
    In the left sidebar of your WordPress dashboard, go to **WP Travel Engine** → **Bookings** and open the affected booking.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Find the Installment Schedule section" title-type="p">
    Scroll down until you see the Installment Schedule area.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click Sync" title-type="p">
    Click the **Sync** button.

The system checks Stripe and updates the booking right away. If Stripe did receive the payment, the status in WP Travel Engine will change to **Completed**.

### The installment option isn't showing up at checkout

This usually comes down to one of three settings.

First, check that **Show Installment Payments** is turned on in your global settings. Then check the trip itself and make sure the trip-specific setting is not set to **Disabled**.

Last, check the trip date against your cut-off days. If the traveler is booking too close to the departure date, the installment option will not appear.

### A traveler says they can't see the installment option

Most of the time, this means the traveler is booking after the cut-off period has started. Cut-off days set the minimum time you need before departure in order to offer scheduled payments.

For example, if your cut-off is 30 days and the trip starts in 25 days, the traveler will only see your normal payment options. This is expected behavior.

### The installment schedule isn't showing in my emails

This usually means the email template is missing the `{installment_schedule}` placeholder.

Open the installment email template and make sure that placeholder is included in the message content. Once it is there, the full schedule can appear in the email.

### A traveler's card was declined but they were charged

This is usually a timing issue, not a double charge. One installment may have been processing successfully while a different installment failed.

Open the booking details and review the installment entries one by one. Then use **Sync** to pull the latest status from Stripe.

## Frequently asked questions

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="Can travelers see the full installment schedule before paying?" default-open="false">
    Yes. Travelers can see the full schedule during checkout before they complete the booking. That includes the payment amounts and due dates.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What happens if someone tries to book after the installment cut-off period?" default-open="false">
    Once the cut-off period has passed, the installment option is hidden at checkout. The traveler will only see the standard payment methods.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Is a Stripe webhook required for installment payments?" default-open="false">
    Yes. A Stripe webhook is required for installment payments to work properly. It lets Stripe notify your site when a payment succeeds or fails.

    Make sure you have added the `invoice.payment_succeeded` and `invoice.payment_failed` events along with the other events your Stripe setup needs.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="How are installment payment amounts calculated?" default-open="false">
    The total trip cost, including fees and taxes, is divided by the number of installments in the selected plan.

    For example, if a trip costs $2,000 and the traveler chooses 4 installments, the system splits that total across the plan automatically.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="How are installment payment dates calculated?" default-open="false">
    The system schedules installment dates from the day the traveler books up to the final payment due date.

    It divides the time between those dates based on the number of installments, so all payments are completed before the trip starts.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can installment plans be changed after a booking is made?" default-open="false">
    No. Once the booking is confirmed, the selected installment plan and payment schedule cannot be changed.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Does installment payment work with all currencies supported by Stripe?" default-open="false">
    Yes. Installment payments work with all currencies supported by Stripe for your account.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can I turn off installments for specific trips?" default-open="false">
    Yes. In the trip-specific Installment Payments setting, choose **Disabled** for that trip.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What happens if a traveler's card is declined for an installment?" default-open="false">
    Stripe retries the payment based on the retry policy you set. If all retries fail, your Subscription Status setting takes over.

    You will also receive an email notification about the failed payment.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Do I need to manually charge each installment?" default-open="false">
    No. Stripe charges each installment automatically on its due date.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can I charge extra for offering installments?" default-open="false">
    Yes. Use **Increment Percentage** to add a percentage-based fee when travelers choose installment payments.

    For example, if you add 5%, that extra amount is included in the installment plan total.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
