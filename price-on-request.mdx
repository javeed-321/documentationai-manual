Some trips need a conversation before a booking. Price on Request is built for those trips.

It replaces the booking widget on your trip page with a pricing card. The card can show a starting price, a few highlights, and a button that sends travelers to an inquiry form. You might use it for custom itineraries, private tours, luxury packages, or any trip where the price depends on who's asking.

It works one trip at a time. The rest of your site stays exactly as it is.

## Key features and benefits

| Feature                                                    | What it does for you                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Per-trip toggle                                            | Turn any trip into a lead capture page before you're ready to publish a price.                      |
| Optional starting price (original + sale + category)       | Give travelers a rough cost idea before they inquire, without locking in a fixed amount.            |
| Trip highlights                                            | Tell travelers what's included and what's flexible, right on the pricing card.                      |
| Customizable section title and button text                 | Match the card's wording to the trip and your brand.                                                |
| Three button actions (inquiry form, custom link, own form) | Send travelers to whichever inquiry channel works best for you, without sending them off your site. |

## What you'll need before you start

Before using this add-on, make sure you have:

- WP Travel Engine installed and activated on your WordPress site. A plugin is a piece of software that adds features to WordPress. WP Travel Engine is a plugin.
- WP Travel Engine updated to its latest version.
- The Price on Request add-on installed and activated. An add-on is a small extension that adds extra features to WP Travel Engine itself.

## Setting up the pricing card

Once the add-on is active, you can configure it on any trip. Go to **Trips** and open the trip you want to set up. On the left side of the trip editor, click **Date and Price**, then select the **Price on Request** tab at the top.

### Turn on Price on Request for this trip

Toggle on **Offer Price on Request**. This hides the booking widget and replaces it with the pricing card on the trip page. The booking widget is the part where travelers pick dates, choose how many people, and check out.

Say you run a 10-day Himalayan trek where the price changes based on group size and season. You don't want a fixed price listed online. This toggle is what makes that possible.

  This setting only affects the trip you're editing. It has no effect on any other trip on your site.

### Choose a heading for the pricing card

The section title is the heading that appears at the top of the pricing card on your trip page. You can change it to anything that fits the trip.

For example, "Request a Custom Itinerary" or "Get a Tailored Quote" both work well. Pick something that matches how you want to talk to your travelers.

### Show a starting price (optional)

If you want to give travelers a rough idea of cost before they reach out, toggle on **Display a Starting Price**. Four fields appear:

- **From** - The label shown before the price. This is the small text that appears above the number.
- **Original price** - The full cost before any discount. It shows with a line through it so travelers can see the savings.
- **Sale price** - The price travelers see highlighted. This field is required when the toggle is on.
- **Price category** - The traveler type this price applies to, like "Adult."

If your trek starts at $2,000 but you sometimes offer a 10% early-bird discount, you'd put $2,000 as the original price and $1,800 as the sale price. Travelers see both numbers and understand the value.

Leave the toggle off if you don't want any price showing on the page.

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
  A starting price tends to reduce back-and-forth. Travelers who reach out already know the ballpark cost.
</Callout>

### Add highlights to the pricing card

Highlights are short bullet points that appear on the pricing card above the inquiry button. They tell travelers what's included, what's flexible, and why the trip is worth contacting you about.

For a safari trip, you might add highlights like "Private vehicle and guide included" or "Choose from 3 accommodation tiers." These give travelers confidence before they reach out.

- Click any field and type your highlight.
- Click **+ Add Trip Highlight** to add more rows.
- Click the delete icon on the right to remove a highlight.

### Write the button text

Enter the label for the inquiry button. This is the text travelers click to get in touch with you.

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
  Labels like "Request a Quote" and "Plan My Trip" tend to get more clicks than generic ones like "Contact Us."

### Choose what happens when travelers click the button

Three options control what the button does. Pick the one that fits how you want to handle inquiries.

#### Open inquiry form

This opens the built-in WP Travel Engine inquiry form right on the trip page. Travelers don't leave the page. No extra setup needed.

This is the easiest option if you want inquiries to come straight into WP Travel Engine.

#### Go to a custom link

This takes travelers to a web address you choose. You might send them to your contact page, a Calendly booking link, or any other page. Two fields appear when you pick this option:

- **Destination URL** - The full web address travelers land on after clicking.
- **Open in a New Tab** - Toggle on to open the link in a new browser tab so travelers don't lose your trip page.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1786948633346-7t1d75rldpq-price-on-request-go-to-custom-link.price-on-request-go-to-custom-link?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="426" alt="" />

#### Use my own form

This opens a form you've already built with another form plugin in a popup window when travelers click the button. It works with Contact Form 7, WPForms, and Gravity Forms, or any plugin that gives you a shortcode.

A shortcode is a short bit of text in square brackets that WordPress replaces with a working form when the page loads. You can usually find it in your form plugin's settings.

- **Form Shortcode** - Paste your form's shortcode here.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1786948660604-zlch4rl959-price-on-request-use-my-own-form.price-on-request-use-my-own-form?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="238" alt="" />

Once you've set everything up, click **Save** in the top right corner of the page. Your pricing card is now live on the trip page.

## Real-world examples

Here are two common situations where Price on Request comes in handy.

### Custom tour with variable pricing

Say you offer private safari packages where the final price depends on group size, travel dates, and accommodation tier. A fixed checkout widget would either undercharge small groups or lose large ones before they even inquire.

Here's how to set that up:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the trip's Price on Request tab" title-type="p">
    Open the trip in your WordPress dashboard and go to **Date and Price**, then **Price on Request**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn on the pricing card" title-type="p">
    Toggle on **Offer Price on Request**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add highlights" title-type="p">
    Add highlights that cover what's included, like private vehicle, guide, and accommodation options.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the button action" title-type="p">
    Set the button action to **Open inquiry form** so travelers can reach out without leaving the page.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save your changes" title-type="p">
    Click **Save**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Publishing a trip before you've set the price

Sometimes you want to publish a new itinerary to gauge interest, but supplier quotes aren't back yet and you can't commit to a price. Price on Request lets the trip go live without a price.

Here's how:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the trip's Price on Request tab" title-type="p">
    Open the trip and go to **Date and Price**, then **Price on Request**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn on the pricing card" title-type="p">
    Toggle on **Offer Price on Request**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Skip the starting price" title-type="p">
    Leave **Display a Starting Price** off so no price appears on the page.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Point the button to your contact channel" title-type="p">
    Set the button action to **Go to a custom link** and point it to your contact page or a Calendly booking link.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save your changes" title-type="p">
    Click **Save**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Troubleshooting

### The Price on Request tab isn't showing up on my trip

Go to **Plugins** on your WordPress dashboard sidebar. Find the Price on Request add-on in the list. Click **Deactivate**, wait a moment, then click **Activate** again. Go back to your trip's Date and Price section and check for the tab.

### I turned on Price on Request but the pricing card isn't showing on my trip page

First, make sure you clicked **Save** in the top right corner after making your changes. If you did save and the card still isn't showing, your site might be showing an older saved copy of the page. A cache is a saved version of your page that loads faster for visitors, but it can sometimes show outdated content. If you have a caching plugin installed, clear the cache from its settings. Then check the trip page again.

### My booking widget disappeared from the trip page

That's expected. When you turn on Price on Request, the booking widget is hidden and replaced with the pricing card. Your packages and existing bookings are not deleted. If you turn off Price on Request later, the booking widget comes back.

### The inquiry button doesn't do anything when I click it

Check which button action you selected in the Price on Request tab. If you chose **Open inquiry form**, the form should appear below the button. If you chose **Go to a custom link**, make sure you entered a valid web address in the Destination URL field. If you chose **Use my own form**, make sure the shortcode is correct. Save your changes and try again.

### The starting price is showing on a trip where I didn't set one

Go back to the trip's **Price on Request** tab and check if the **Display a Starting Price** toggle is on. If it is and you don't want a price showing, turn it off and save the trip.

## Frequently asked questions

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="Does Price on Request affect other trips on my site?" default-open="false">
    No. Only the trip you configure it on will show the pricing card.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What happens to my existing packages if I turn on Price on Request?" default-open="false">
    The booking widget is hidden from the trip page, but your packages and existing bookings are not deleted. Turn off Price on Request later, and the booking widget returns.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can I use Price on Request and standard packages on the same trip?" default-open="false">
    No. Turning on Price on Request hides the booking widget for that trip. Travelers can only see the pricing card and inquiry button.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Which form does the inquiry button use?" default-open="false">
    The built-in WP Travel Engine inquiry form. You don't need to set anything up for it.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can I show a starting price but still collect inquiries instead of bookings?" default-open="false">
    Yes. Turn on both **Offer Price on Request** and **Display a Starting Price**. Travelers see the price and click the button to inquire. No checkout happens.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can I use Price on Request on some trips and regular booking on others?" default-open="false">
    Yes. The setting works per trip. You can have one trip with Price on Request and another with the standard booking widget.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What happens if I turn off Price on Request later?" default-open="false">
    The pricing card disappears and the booking widget comes back. Any packages you had set up are still there.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
