## Waitlist

Let travelers join a waitlist when a trip is full, so you still capture booking interest after seats run out. The Waitlist add-on is a plugin. A plugin is a small extension that adds features to WordPress. This one adds a waitlist option to your trip pages when travelers cannot book right away.

When a seat opens up, you already have interested travelers ready to contact. This can happen when someone cancels. It can also happen when you release more capacity or add a new departure date. This works especially well for trips that fill up fast or only have a small number of spots.

<Video src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/MB_PXcdyJWo" title="Waitlist add-on overview video" width="100%" height="600" />

## Key features

- **Join Waitlist** - travelers can sign up for trips that are full
- **Availability Modes** - show the waitlist only when the trip is full, or from the very start
- **Waitlist Capacity** - set a limit on how many people can join the list
- **Booked and Available Seats Display** - show how many people have joined and how many spots are left
- **Email Notifications** - automatically email travelers and trip admins when seats open up
- **Admin Management** - view, email, and remove waitlist sign-ups from your dashboard

These are the main parts of the add-on. Next, you'll set it up for all your trips.

## Setting up the Waitlist add-on

These settings apply to all your trips at once. You can change one trip or one date later if needed.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">

What you'll need before you start: the Waitlist add-on must be installed and activated.

</Callout>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Waitlist settings" title-type="p">

  This is where you control how the waitlist works across all your trips.

  On the left sidebar of your WordPress dashboard, click **WP Travel Engine**. Then click **Settings**. Then click **Extensions**. Then click **Waitlist**.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Confirm the Waitlist settings panel is visible" title-type="p">

  This confirms the add-on is active and ready to configure.

  You should now see the Waitlist settings page. If the settings panel opens, the add-on is active and ready.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## General settings

These settings control when the waitlist appears and what travelers see. You can set them in any order.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-20.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="General Waitlist settings screen" />

- **Show Waitlist**: Turn this ON to make the waitlist available on all your trips. When it's off, travelers won't see any waitlist option.
- **Section Title**: This is the heading at the top of the small form that pops up when a traveler joins the waitlist. Use something clear like `Waitlist` or `Join the Waitlist`.
- **Short Notice Message**: This is a short line of text that appears on your trip page when the waitlist is active. Try something like `Waitlist open - reserve your spot today!` so travelers know what is happening.
- **Waitlist Availability Mode**: This controls when the **Join Waitlist** button appears on your trip pages.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="When Trip is Fully Booked">

  The waitlist button only shows up after every seat for the trip or departure date is sold out. Travelers can join the waitlist once the trip is full. This is the most common choice.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="From the Start">

  The waitlist button appears right away, even if seats are still open. Travelers can join the waitlist but cannot book the trip. Use this when you want to measure early interest, collect leads, or promote a trip before opening bookings.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-43-1024x538.png" width="1024" height="538" alt="Join Waitlist button on the trip page" />

Say you run a popular Everest Base Camp trek that fills up every spring. You'd choose `When Trip is Fully Booked` so travelers can join once seats run out. But if you're planning a new cultural tour for next season and want to see how many people are interested before setting dates, choose `From the Start`.

You can always change this later. Next, you can decide how many people to collect and what details to show on the trip page.

## Capacity and display settings

These settings help you control how many sign-ups you collect and what travelers can see on the trip page.

- **Waitlist Capacity**: This sets the maximum number of travelers who can join the waitlist. Once the limit is reached, no one else can sign up. Leave it blank if you don't want a limit.

Say you run a 12-person group trek and it sold out. You might set the waitlist capacity to 6. That way you collect interest from a manageable number of people, not hundreds of sign-ups you can't follow up with.

- **Display Waitlist Booked Seats**: Turn this ON to show how many travelers have already joined. This updates automatically.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-30.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="Waitlist booked seats count displayed on the trip page" />

- **Display Waitlist Available Seats**: Turn this ON to show how many waitlist spots are left. This updates automatically.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-29.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="Waitlist available seats count displayed on the trip page" />

- **Popup Disclaimer Text**: This is a short note below the waitlist form. Use it to set expectations, like `By joining the waitlist, you'll receive an email if a spot becomes available.`

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-34-1024x118.png" width="1024" height="118" alt="Popup disclaimer text field for the waitlist form" />
<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-32.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="Waitlist popup form showing the disclaimer text" />

- **Button Label, Background Color, and Text Color**: Change the text on the **Join Waitlist** button and its colors to match your website. Make sure the button text is easy to read against the background.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-35.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="Waitlist button label and color customization fields" />

- **Enable ReCaptcha**: Turn this ON to add a spam check. reCAPTCHA is a free tool from Google that tells the difference between a real person and an automated spam program. It stops fake sign-ups from clogging up your waitlist.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-38.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="Enable ReCaptcha toggle in Waitlist settings" />
<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-39-963x1024.png" width="963" height="1024" alt="reCAPTCHA challenge shown in the waitlist popup" />

<Callout kind="info">

This feature only works if you've set up your reCAPTCHA keys first. See the [Setting up reCAPTCHA to block spam](#setting-up-recaptcha-to-block-spam) section below.

</Callout>

- **Link Availability Duration**: This sets how long, in hours, the booking link stays valid when you email a waitlisted traveler about an open spot. After this time, the link stops working.

If you set this to 24 hours, a traveler who gets an email about an open spot has one day to book before the link stops working. Choose a time that gives travelers enough chance to check their email and decide.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-40.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="Link Availability Duration setting in hours" />

Click **Save Settings** when you're done. Your waitlist is ready. If you turned on reCAPTCHA, the next section shows how to connect it.

## Setting up reCAPTCHA to block spam

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">

What you'll need before you start: a free Google account.

</Callout>

To use reCAPTCHA on your waitlist forms, you need to connect your site to Google first.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-2.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="reCAPTCHA setup overview" />

<Steps>
  <Step title="Get your keys from Google" title-type="p">

  This gives your website the details it needs to use Google's spam check.

  Go to the [Google reCAPTCHA Admin Console](https://www.google.com/recaptcha/admin/create). Enter a name for your site, like `My Travel Site`. Choose the version you want. Score based, or v3, works quietly in the background. Challenge, or v2, asks users to complete a small task like picking out traffic lights. Enter your website's domain name. After you submit, Google will give you two pieces of text called a Site Key and a Secret Key. Copy both of them. You'll need them in the next step.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Add the keys to WP Travel Engine" title-type="p">

  This step connects Google's spam check to your waitlist form.

  On the left sidebar of your WordPress dashboard, go to **WP Travel Engine**. Then go to **Settings**. Then go to **Extensions**. Then go to **reCAPTCHA**. Select the version, v2 or v3, that matches what you chose on Google's site. Paste your Site Key and Secret Key into the fields. Click **Save Settings**.

  <Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-47-scaled.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="reCAPTCHA settings screen with Site Key and Secret Key fields" />

  </Step>
</Steps>

That's it. Your waitlist form is now protected from spam. Next, you can change the email travelers receive when a spot opens up.

## Customizing the waitlist email

When a seat opens up, the Waitlist add-on sends an email automatically. You can customize what it says. Find these settings on the same Waitlist settings page, in the Email section.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-18.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="Waitlist email template editor" />

- **Email Subject**: This is the subject line travelers see in their inbox. You can use personalization tags, explained below, to make it specific, like `Spots are now available for your trip!`
- **Email Message**: This is the body of the email. Use the text editor to add your message, formatting, and images. Write something friendly that tells the traveler a spot opened up and gives them a clear next step.
- **Personalization dropdown**: This lets you insert personalization tags into your email. These are shortcodes. A shortcode is a small placeholder that gets replaced automatically when the email is sent. For example, when the email goes out, the trip name tag becomes the actual name of your trip.

| Tag | What it displays |
| --- | --- |
| `{traveler_name}` | The name of the customer. |
| `{trip_name}` | The name of the specific trip. |
| `{trip_date}` | The departure date the traveler is interested in. |
| `{site_name}` | Your website's name. |
| `{booking_link}` | The unique URL that allows the traveler to confirm their booking. |

<Callout kind="info">

The `booking_link` tag only works for the number of hours you set in the Link Availability Duration setting. After that, it stops working.

</Callout>

A good email can make the difference between a traveler booking right away or letting the spot slip. Next, you can change the waitlist for one trip without affecting the rest of your site.

## Changing the waitlist for a single trip

The global settings you set up earlier apply to all your trips. But sometimes you want one trip or one departure date to work differently.

<Callout kind="info">

What you'll need before you start: you should have at least one trip created with departure dates.

</Callout>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a trip's Dates settings" title-type="p">

  This is where you manage the dates travelers can book for a specific trip.

  On the trip page, go to **WP Travel Engine**. Then go to **Trip Settings**. Then go to **Date and Price**. Then go to **Edit Pricing and Dates**. Then open **Dates**.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Edit a specific date" title-type="p">

  This takes you to the settings for one departure date.

  Click on the date you want to change.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the Waitlist Status" title-type="p">

  This controls whether the waitlist shows up for this specific date.

  Find the **Waitlist Status** setting and choose one:

  - **Disabled**: The waitlist is turned off for this date. Travelers won't see it.
  - **When Trip is Fully Booked**: The waitlist appears only after all seats for this date are sold out.
  - **From the Start**: The waitlist appears right away, even if seats are still open.

  <Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-15.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="Waitlist Status setting for a specific trip date" />

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="info">

The option you pick here overrides the global setting for this specific date only. Your other trips and dates are not affected.

</Callout>

Say most of your trips use `When Trip is Fully Booked`, but you have one special New Year's Eve fireworks cruise where you want to collect interest from day one. You'd set that specific date's Waitlist Status to `From the Start` without changing anything else.

That gives you flexibility when one departure needs different treatment. Next, here are two real examples that make the setup easier to picture.

## Managing your waitlist submissions

This is where you see everyone who joined your waitlists and where you can contact them. You can also release seats privately to people on the list.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Upcoming Tours" title-type="p">

  This is where you'll find all your trips and their waitlist entries.

  On the left sidebar of your WordPress dashboard, go to **WP Travel Engine**. Then click **Upcoming Tours**. You'll see a list of your trips and departure dates.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Open Waitlist Details" title-type="p">

  This shows everyone who signed up for a specific departure.

  Click **Waitlist Details** on the trip you want to check.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Release seats privately" title-type="p">

  This lets you offer open spots to waitlisted travelers before anyone else sees them on your site.

  Set the **Total Released Seats** number to open up spots for waitlisted travelers. These seats stay hidden on your website. The only way to book them is through the private link you send by email. This gives you full control over who gets first access.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Review the waitlist entries" title-type="p">

  This helps you decide who to contact and what each traveler asked for.

  You can see each traveler's name, how many people they're booking for, their contact details, any message they left, and their current status.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Email a traveler directly" title-type="p">

  This saves time when you're ready to offer a spot.

  Click the Mail icon next to any entry. A window pops up with the recipient, subject, and message already filled in from your email template. Review it, make any changes, and send.

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/image-42.png" width="1736" height="948" alt="Waitlist submissions list in the Upcoming Tours dashboard" />

Say someone cancels on your 7-day Annapurna Circuit trek two weeks before departure. You go to **Upcoming Tours**, find that trip, click **Waitlist Details**, and release 1 seat. Then you email the first person on the waitlist with a private booking link. They get first access to that spot before anyone else sees it on your website.

## Troubleshooting

Here are some common issues and how to fix them. If you're hitting a problem not listed here, skip to the support note at the bottom of this page.

### The Join Waitlist button isn't showing up on my trip page

Check that **Show Waitlist** is turned ON in your global settings. Then check the **Waitlist Availability Mode**. If it's set to `When Trip is Fully Booked` and the trip still has open seats, the button won't appear yet. It only shows up once the trip is full.

### Travelers can't book my trip anymore

If you selected `From the Start` mode, travelers can only join the waitlist. They cannot book. Switch to `When Trip is Fully Booked` in your settings if you want booking to work normally and the waitlist to appear only when seats run out.

### I can't see any waitlist submissions

Go to **WP Travel Engine**. Then go to **Upcoming Tours**. Click **Waitlist Details** on the departure you're checking. Make sure the trip has at least one departure date with the waitlist turned on.

### The reCAPTCHA isn't working on my waitlist form

You need to set up reCAPTCHA keys first. Follow the steps in the [Setting up reCAPTCHA to block spam](#setting-up-recaptcha-to-block-spam) section above to get your keys from Google and add them to WP Travel Engine.

### The booking link I sent to a waitlisted traveler expired too fast

Increase the **Link Availability Duration** in your global Waitlist settings. The value is in hours, so try increasing it to give travelers more time to check their email and book the open spot.

### My waitlist form is getting spam sign-ups

Turn on **Enable ReCaptcha** in your Waitlist settings. This adds a spam check to the form. You'll need to set up reCAPTCHA keys first. See the [Setting up reCAPTCHA to block spam](#setting-up-recaptcha-to-block-spam) section above.

If your issue matches one of these, you should be back on track. If not, the FAQ covers a few more common questions.

## Frequently asked questions

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="Can travelers book the trip if I turn on 'From the Start' mode?" default-open="false">

  No. When `From the Start` is selected, travelers can only join the waitlist. Booking is not available until you switch the Waitlist Availability Mode to `When Trip is Fully Booked`.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="How do I limit how many travelers can join the waitlist?" default-open="false">

  Set a maximum number in the **Waitlist Capacity** field in your global Waitlist settings. Leave it blank if you don't want a limit.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Where can I see all the waitlist sign-ups?" default-open="false">

  Go to **WP Travel Engine**. Then go to **Upcoming Tours**. Click **Waitlist Details** on the departure you want to check. You can view, email, and remove waitlist entries from there.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Does the waitlist work for each departure date separately?" default-open="false">

  Yes. Each departure date has its own waitlist setting. You can control it for each date independently.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Do I need to set up the waitlist for every trip individually?" default-open="false">

  No. The global settings apply to all trips by default. You only need to change individual trip settings if you want something different for a specific departure date.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Will travelers know their position on the waitlist?" default-open="false">

  If you turn on **Display Waitlist Booked Seats** and **Display Waitlist Available Seats**, travelers can see how many people have joined and how many spots are left. They won't see their exact position in line, though.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can I email someone on the waitlist before seats open up?" default-open="false">

  Yes. Go to **WP Travel Engine**. Then go to **Upcoming Tours**. Click **Waitlist Details** and use the Mail icon next to any traveler's name to send them an email directly.

  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
