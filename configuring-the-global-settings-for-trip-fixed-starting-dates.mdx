## Configuring the global settings for Trip Fixed Starting Dates

These settings control how fixed departure dates work across every trip on your site. You only need to set them up once. After that, every trip you create follows these rules unless you change something for one specific trip. If this is one of your first plugin setups, take your time. You can come back and adjust any of these settings later.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false" color="#10b981">
  You'll need the Trip Fixed Starting Dates add-on activated before you start. A plugin (an add-on that extends what WP Travel Engine can do) needs to be turned on before its settings appear.
</Callout>

This first step takes you to the right screen so all the settings below are in one place.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Fixed Starting Dates settings" title-type="p">
    In your WordPress dashboard, look at the left sidebar. This is the admin area you see after logging in.

    Find **WP Travel Engine** and click **Settings**. Then click **Extensions**, **Fixed Starting Dates**, and **General**.

    That page contains all the settings covered below.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Enable Trip Fixed Starting Dates for trips

This is the main switch for the add-on. Turn it on to show the Fixed Starting Dates section on your trip pages. Turn it off and that section disappears from all trips.

**Fixed Starting Dates Section Title:** This is the heading travelers see above your departure dates list. You can type any title that fits your business. "Available Departures" and "Trip Dates" are both clear choices. Leave it blank if you want to keep the default heading.

If you run guided treks with set departure days, a clear title helps travelers spot the dates quickly. That is especially useful when the trip page also includes itineraries, maps, and package details.

Once this is on, you can decide how the booking window behaves.

### Booking Modal options

These settings control what happens after a traveler clicks **Book Now** next to a date.

When a traveler clicks **Book Now**, a small pop-up window opens to complete the booking. You can choose how much information shows in that window.

**Skip Date Selection in Booking Modal:** Turn this on to hide the date field in the booking pop-up. The traveler already clicked a date from the list, so they do not need to pick it again. The system already knows which date they chose.

This makes the booking flow feel shorter. It also cuts down on mistakes.

Say you run a weekend hiking trip with three departure dates. A traveler clicks **Book Now** on the March 15 date. With this setting on, the pop-up opens straight to the booking form. The traveler does not have to choose the same date a second time.

### Enable FSD below the Banner

This setting controls where the departure dates section appears on your trip pages. That changes what travelers notice first.

**Show FSD below Banner:** Turn this on to place the departure dates section right below the main banner image at the top of the trip page. This is the default position for all trips.

If you turn it off for a specific trip in that trip's own settings, that trip uses its own preference instead. In that case, the dates section appears lower on the page. It shows below the main trip content.

If dates are the main thing your travelers need to see, this placement works well. It is especially useful for fixed-date group tours where people want to check departures before reading everything else.

### Trip Expiry Message

This message appears after a trip passes its expiry date. It tells travelers the trip is no longer available.

You can write this message in your own words. "This trip has ended" and "Bookings closed for this season" both work well. Keep it short so the message is clear at a glance.

Say you run a summer trek that finishes in September. After the last departure is gone, you do not want people trying to book it in October. This message gives them a clear answer instead of leaving them guessing.

### Display Options

These settings control what information appears in the departure dates table on your trip pages. You can choose whether to show availability labels, prices, and remaining seats. Think of it as choosing which columns to display in a spreadsheet - each one gives travelers another piece of helpful information.

**Date Availability Label:** These are small tags like "Guaranteed," "Limited," or "Available" that appear next to each departure date. They help travelers understand availability quickly. Turn this off if you prefer a cleaner table.

If you have a trek with 12 seats and only 2 left, a "Limited" label helps travelers decide sooner. It gives them useful context without making them open the booking form first.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787568144832-8ngav9mkjok-fsd-available-dates-frontend.fsd-available-dates-frontend?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="992" height="741" alt="" />

**Price:** This shows the price for each departure date directly in the table. By default, it shows the price of your primary package, which is the first package you created for that trip.

If you sell Standard and Premium packages, travelers will see the Standard price in the table. That gives them a starting price before they open the full booking form.

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787568277048-4nblz7uf4in-fsd-price-frontend.fsd-price-frontend?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="718" alt="" />

**Space left:** This shows how many seats are still available for each date. Turn it on if you want travelers to see at a glance whether a trip is filling up.

Once enabled, you can choose how the seat count is displayed:

- **Primary Package:** Shows the number of seats left in the primary package only.
- **Total Seats:** Shows the combined number of seats left across all packages for that date.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false" color="#10b981">
  Say your General Settings set 10 seats total, and you created two packages: Standard with 4 seats and Premium with 3 seats. With Primary Package, the table shows 4 seats (from Standard). With Total Seats, it shows 7 seats (from both packages combined). Using Total Seats is recommended so customers see the overall availability.
</Callout>

**Number of Trip Dates per pagination:** This controls how many departure dates appear on one page before the rest move to the next page. A shortcode is a small piece of text in square brackets that tells WordPress where to display something. When you use the `[WTE_TRIPS_FIXED_STARTING_DATES]` shortcode to show your trips, this setting keeps the list from getting too long.

If you have a trip with 20 departure dates, setting this to 10 means travelers see the first 10 dates, then click to see the next 10. That keeps the page tidy and easier to scan.

#### Configuring Departure Date status

These settings let you show travelers how full each trip is by using labels and seat counts. The colors help people spot availability quickly.

<Video uid="71a480d8-13b8-4ac7-9f28-8ac684ca4e78" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/2Zza0w1yh9E" render-type="iframe" width="642" height="361" controls="true" allow-full-screen="true" style="width: 642px; height: auto; cursor: pointer;" />

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787569159408-akeofomzpgc-fsd-departure-date-status.fsd-departure-date-status?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="772" height="554" alt="" />

- **Display Booked Seats:** Shows how many seats have already been booked for each date. This can help travelers see which departures are popular.
- **Display Status:** Shows the availability labels, such as "Available" or "Sold Out," next to each date.
  - **Available:** This label appears when a trip still has open seats. It uses a green color by default. The percentage is fixed at 0% booked.
  - **Sold Out:** This label appears when every seat is taken. It uses a red color by default. The percentage is fixed at 100% booked.
  - **Add Status:** Use this to create your own label. For example, you could add "Limited" when 80% of seats are filled. You choose the label, the color, and the percentage for custom statuses.

<Callout kind="note" collapsed="false">
  Say you run a 10-seat trek. You could set it up like this: 0% booked shows "Available" in green. 80% booked shows "Limited" in yellow. 100% booked shows "Sold Out" in red. Travelers see the color change as seats fill up, which encourages earlier bookings.
</Callout>

### Adding excluded dates

Excluded dates let you block specific dates without editing every trip one by one. This is useful for public holidays, staff shortages, office closures, or days when a guide is unavailable.

<Video uid="025a2786-04d8-473a-84f0-5929b86a1fca" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/uYhS-p9RYoI" render-type="iframe" width="644" height="362" controls="true" allow-full-screen="true" style="width: 644px; height: auto; cursor: pointer;" />

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787569369763-74a5nsd6ibq-fsd-excluded-dates.fsd-excluded-dates?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="510" alt="" />

Here's how to set up an excluded date, step by step.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Exclude Dates" title-type="p">
    This takes you to the area where all blocked dates are managed.

    Open the settings under Fixed Starting Dates and go to the **Exclude Dates** tab.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Select the dates to block" title-type="p">
    Start here when you know the exact day or date range you want to close.

    In Select Dates, pick one or more dates, or check "Select a range of dates using the range calendar" to block a date range at once, then click **Add Dates**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose a block behavior" title-type="p">
    This choice decides how strict the block should be.

    You have three options:

    **Block Entire Trip Duration**

    No trip that includes this date anywhere in its schedule can be booked. This is the strictest option. Use it for holidays when nothing should run at all.

    **Block Start Date Only**

    Trips can't start on this date. But if a trip started earlier and is still running on this date, it stays bookable. Use this for peak days when you don't want new trips starting but ongoing ones are fine.

    **Block Specific Time Slots**

    This blocks only the specific start times you choose on this date. Other time slots on the same day stay open. This works well for high-frequency tours, like hourly city walks, where you want to stop overbooking on one slot without shutting down the whole day.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Select time slots (if applicable)" title-type="p">
    Only use this step if you chose "Block Specific Time Slots."

    If you picked "Block Specific Time Slots," add the start times to block under Select Time Slots. Click **Add Time** to block more than one slot on the same date.

    <Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787569591416-fda3z7em3t6-fsd-slect-time-slots.fsd-slect-time-slots?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="288" alt="" />
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose which trips the exclusion applies to" title-type="p">
    This setting matters when a blocked date should affect some trips but not others.

    Under Apply Exclusion To, choose:

    - **All Trips** - blocks the date for every trip on your site.
    - **Specific Trips** - blocks the date only for the trips you choose.
    - **All Except Selected Trips** - blocks the date for every trip except the ones you choose.

    <Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787569635378-odv0fjbfcbc-fsd-apply-exclusion-to.fsd-apply-exclusion-to?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="288" alt="" />
  </Step>

  <Step title="Select specific trips (if applicable)" title-type="p">
    Use this when the exclusion should apply only to certain trips.

    If you chose Specific Trips or All Except Selected Trips, search for and select the trips in Trips to Exclude.

    <Callout kind="info" collapsed="false" color="#8b5cf6">
      Use "All Except Selected Trips" when most of your trips should stay closed on a date but a few need to stay bookable, like a private charter that still runs on a public holiday.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save the exclusion" title-type="p">
    This final step makes the block live on your site.

    Optionally enable **Repeat Yearly** to block the same date every year, then click **Save Settings**. Click Details on any saved entry to review or change it later, or use the delete icon to remove it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Examples of using excluded dates:**

- Blocking public holidays when your office is closed (Block Entire Trip Duration).
- Preventing trips from starting on certain peak days while allowing ongoing trips (Block Start Date Only).
- Excluding peak days when guides are unavailable.
- Blocking a single overbooked departure time while keeping other time slots open on the same day (Block Specific Time Slots).
- Marking company retreat days where no trips should be booked.

<Callout kind="alert" collapsed="false">
  The scope of an excluded date depends on what you choose under Apply Exclusion To. If you set a date to All Trips, no trip can be booked on that day, even if individual trips have seats available. If scoped to Specific Trips or All Except Selected Trips, only the chosen trips are affected.
</Callout>

Once excluded dates are in place, you can decide how fixed dates appear on your archive pages.

### Trip Archive

Your trip archive page is where travelers browse all your trips. These settings control how departure dates appear on the trip cards on that page.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Screenshot_100825_024840_PM-1024x502.jpg" width="1024" height="502" alt="Trip Archive display settings" />

- **Show trips with fixed starting dates:** Turn this on so only trips that have fixed departure dates appear on your trip archive pages. This keeps the archive focused on trips with scheduled departures.
- **Show Dates on Cards:** Turn this on to show departure dates directly on each trip card. Turn it off to hide them.
- **No. of Dates on Cards:** Choose how many upcoming departure dates appear on each trip card. For example, setting this to 3 shows the next three dates. If a trip has more than that, travelers can open the trip page to see the rest.

If you run many scheduled departures, these settings help people compare trips faster. They can see dates before opening each trip one by one.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/image-8-1024x587.png" width="1024" height="587" alt="Trip cards showing upcoming departure dates" />

This section helps when a trip is not built around scheduled departures. You might offer private treks, custom family tours, or trips that need a quote before booking.

- **Private Trip Form:** Add a form shortcode here so travelers can contact you. You can use the Enquiry Form Shortcode or any other form plugin you have installed. The form appears next to the departure dates section, so travelers can ask a question or request a quote without leaving the page.

This works well for custom departures, school groups, or trips with add-ons like gear rental and private transport.

### Availability Labels

Availability labels are small colored tags that appear next to your departure dates. They tell travelers at a glance whether a trip is guaranteed to run, has limited spots, or is fully booked. You can create your own labels and pick a color for each one.

<Video src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/aIwu58_ndbk" render-type="video" width="100%" height="600" controls="true" allow-full-screen="true" />

The plugin comes with three labels ready to use: Guaranteed, Available, and Limited. You can add more by clicking **Add More Labels**.

For example, if you run multi-day treks and want a "Filling Fast" label for trips that are 70% booked, you can create that label, choose an orange color, and assign it to any trip date.

- **Color Picker:** Each label gets its own color. Pick colors travelers understand quickly. Green for "Available," yellow for "Limited," and red for "Sold Out" is a familiar pattern.
- **Actions:** You can remove any custom label you no longer need. When you delete a label, it is also removed from any trips where it was assigned. The "Choose Label" option on those trips resets to empty.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Screenshot_100825_033313_PM-1024x822.jpg" width="1024" height="822" alt="Availability Labels management screen" />

The Trip Departure Calendar shows all your upcoming trip departures in one place. Instead of browsing trip by trip, travelers can scan dates across your whole site and choose what suits them best.

Why this matters: it keeps people focused on trips they can still book.

##### Using shortcodes

Once you've saved your settings, you can place the departure calendar on any page of your site using a shortcode. You copy it into a page, and WordPress displays the calendar there.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/shortcode-section.png" width="1024" height="600" alt="Departure Calendar shortcode section" />

**Simple shortcode**

Copy this shortcode and paste it into any page or post:

`[WPTravelEngine_Trip_Departure_Calendar]`

It displays the calendar exactly as you configured it in the global settings. No extra setup is needed.

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/shortcode-paste-in-a-page.png" width="1024" height="600" alt="Shortcode pasted into a page" />

<Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/departure-calendar-1.png" width="1024" height="600" alt="Departure calendar rendered on the frontend" />

**Advanced shortcode (optional)**

Use the advanced shortcode when you want one page to behave differently from your global settings. These extra settings override the global settings for that one page only.

For example, you could keep a grid layout across your site but show a list layout on a dedicated departures page.

```typescript
[WPTravelEngine_Trip_Departure_Calendar layout="list-one" display_contact_us="yes" display_available_seats="yes" display_only_booked_trips="yes" display_available_trips_only="yes" no_of_trips="24" exclude="trip_id1,trip_id2" include="trip_id1,trip_id2" default_year="20xx" default_month="all"]
```

| Attribute                      | Accepts                        | Description                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `layout`                       | `grid`, `list-one`, `list-two` | Force that layout for this page.                                                                    |
| `display_contact_us`           | `yes` / `no`                   | Show or hide the Contact Us button.                                                                 |
| `display_available_seats`      | `yes` / `no`                   | Show or hide Spots Left / Sold Out badges.                                                          |
| `display_only_booked_trips`    | `yes` / `no`                   | Show only trips with at least one confirmed booking.                                                |
| `display_available_trips_only` | `yes` / `no`                   | Hide fully booked (sold-out) trips.                                                                 |
| `no_of_trips`                  | 3–48                           | How many trips load initially.                                                                      |
| `exclude`                      | comma-separated trip IDs       | Trips to leave out, e.g., `6380,6783`.                                                              |
| `include`                      | comma-separated trip IDs       | Trips to display exclusively.                                                                       |
| `default_year`                 | `20xx` or a specific year      | `20xx` shows the current year; a specific year (e.g., `2027`) shows trips for that year by default. |
| `default_month`                | `all` or 1–12                  | `all` shows trips from all months; a number shows a specific month (e.g., `6` for June).            |

**How to find the trip ID**

If you want to use the `exclude` or `include` options in the advanced shortcode, you'll need your trip IDs. A trip ID is the number WordPress uses to identify each trip behind the scenes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Trips" title-type="p">
    This takes you to the list of all trips on your site.

    Go to **Trips** in your WordPress dashboard. You'll find it on the left sidebar.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Hover over a trip" title-type="p">
    This lets you see the trip link without opening the trip.

    Move your mouse over the trip you want to find the ID for. Don't click it. Just hover.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Read the URL preview" title-type="p">
    The trip ID appears inside the link preview.

    Look at the bottom-left corner of your browser. You'll see a small link appear. It contains something like `post=1234`. The number after the equals sign is your trip ID. In this example, the trip ID is 1234.

    <Image src="https://docs.wptravelengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/trip-id-1.png" width="1024" height="600" alt="Trip ID visible in the URL preview" />
  </Step>

  <Step title="Use the IDs in the shortcode" title-type="p">
    Once you have the ID, you can filter which trips appear in the calendar.

    Copy the trip ID numbers and paste them into the shortcode. If you're listing more than one, separate them with commas and no spaces. For example: `exclude="1234,5678"`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  If you add an attribute to the shortcode but leave its value empty (for example, `no_of_trips=""`), the calendar will use the value from your global settings instead.
</Callout>

### Saving settings

Once you've adjusted the options you want, scroll to the bottom of the page and click **Save Settings**. Your changes do not take effect until you save them.

If you are working through several sections in one sitting, save after each major change. That way you do not lose your work if your browser closes or refreshes.

### Troubleshooting

#### I changed the settings, but nothing changed on my trip page

Make sure you clicked **Save Settings** at the bottom of the page. If you changed settings but did not save, nothing updates on your site. Also check that the Trip Fixed Starting Dates add-on is activated. Go to **Plugins** in your WordPress dashboard and look for the Fixed Starting Dates add-on in the list. If it is not active, click **Activate**.

#### I blocked some dates, but travelers can still see or book them

Check what you selected under **Apply Exclusion To**. If you chose **Specific Trips**, the exclusion only affects those trips. If you want the date blocked everywhere, switch it to **All Trips** and save again.

#### I added the departure calendar shortcode, but no calendar is showing

Make sure you copied the full shortcode including the square brackets. The shortcode should look like this: `[WPTravelEngine_Trip_Departure_Calendar]`. Check that you pasted it into the page content area, not into a heading or an image block. If you're using the WordPress block editor, paste it into a Shortcode block or a Paragraph block.

#### The sticky filter bar is not staying at the top when I scroll

The sticky filter does not work while you're logged in as an admin. This is normal. To see it working, open your site in a private or incognito browser window. You can also log out of WordPress and visit the page as a regular traveler would.

#### My trip cards are not showing departure dates

Check that **Show Dates on Cards** is turned on in the Trip Archive settings. Also make sure the trip has at least one fixed departure date assigned to it. You can check this by editing the trip and looking at the Date and Price section.

### FAQs

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="Can I set different settings for individual trips?" default-open="false">
    Yes. The global settings apply to all trips by default. But you can override most of them inside a specific trip. Go to the trip's edit page and look for the Date and Price section. Changes you make there only affect that one trip.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Do excluded dates affect trips that are already booked?" default-open="false">
    No. Excluded dates prevent new bookings on those dates. If someone already booked a trip before you added the exclusion, their booking stays in place. The exclusion only stops future bookings.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can I show different availability labels for different trips?" default-open="false">
    Yes. The labels you create in the global settings are available to all trips. When you assign a date to a trip, you can pick which label to use for that specific date. So one trip could show "Guaranteed" while another shows "Limited."
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What happens when a trip date is fully booked?" default-open="false">
    The date automatically shows as "Sold Out" (or whatever label you set for 100% booked). Travelers can still see the date, but they can't book it. They can browse other available dates on the same trip.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Can I put the departure calendar on my homepage?" default-open="false">
    Yes. Copy the shortcode and paste it into any page, including your homepage. The calendar will show all your upcoming trip departures right there. You can also use the advanced shortcode to show only certain trips on the homepage.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
