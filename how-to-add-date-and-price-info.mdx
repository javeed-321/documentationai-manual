## Why packages and pricing categories matter

Packages and pricing categories help you offer the same trip in more than one way. A **package** is one version of your trip with its own price and settings. For example, a 5-day trek might have a **Standard Trek** package and a **Deluxe Package** with upgraded lodging or extra inclusions.

A **pricing category** is a price tier inside that package. Common examples are **Adult**, **Child**, and **Senior**. You might also use categories for special rates, such as a local resident price or a student discount.

Offering more than one price point gives more travelers a way to book with you. Some guests want the lowest cost option. Others are happy to pay more for added comfort, private transport, or gear rental.

To find these settings, open **Trips** in the left sidebar of your WordPress dashboard, then open the trip you want to edit. On the trip editing screen, look for the section labeled **Date and Price** on the right side of the page.

Once you know where this area lives, you can start by adding your first package.

## Add a new package

What you'll need before you start: a trip opened for editing.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Date and Price section" title-type="p">
    This is where you control the booking options customers will see on the trip page.

    While editing your trip, look through the settings sections on the right side of the page. Find **Date and Price** and open it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click the Add New Packages button" title-type="p">
    This creates a new package entry for this trip.

    Click **Add New Packages**. A new package row will appear in the section.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Name the package" title-type="p">
    A clear name helps you stay organized and helps customers compare options later.

    Enter a package name such as **Standard Trek**, **Deluxe Package**, or **Private Day Tour**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click Edit Pricing and Date to open the package settings" title-type="p">
    This opens the full settings for that package.

    Click **Edit Pricing and Date** to expand the pricing and booking date options for the package you just created.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Now that your package exists, the next step is to decide which pricing categories you want to use inside it.

## Set up your pricing categories

Pricing categories are the price tiers inside a package, such as **Adult**, **Child**, or **Senior**. You create these once for your whole site, and then they become available when you price any trip.

You can create them at **Dashboard > Trips > Pricing Categories**. In WordPress, that means opening **Trips** from the left sidebar and choosing **Pricing Categories**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to the Pricing Categories page" title-type="p">
    This is where you create the labels you will reuse across your trips.

    In your WordPress dashboard, click **Trips** in the left sidebar. Then click **Pricing Categories**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a new category" title-type="p">
    Start here if you need a new price tier for your trips.

    Click **Add New Pricing Category** if that option is shown, or fill in the new category form on the page.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Fill in the category details" title-type="p">
    These fields help you keep pricing organized across your site.

    - **Name** - Enter the customer-facing label, such as **Adult**, **Child**, or **Senior**.
    - **Slug** - This is a system label that WordPress creates automatically from the name. You can leave it as it is.
    - **Parent category** - Most pricing categories do not need a parent category. You can usually leave this blank.
    - **Description** - Use this for your own reference. Travelers will not see it.
    - **Primary pricing category** checkbox - This highlights the main price customers see first. Most tour operators set **Adult** as the primary category.
    - **Age group** - Add the age range that fits the category. For example, **Child** might be ages 5 to 12.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Once your categories are ready, you can assign real prices to each one inside your package.

## Set prices for each category

Now you can give each category its own price inside the package. This is where your trip starts to match how you actually sell - per adult, per child, per group, or with a special seasonal offer.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a pricing category for the package" title-type="p">
    Pick the category you want to price first.

    Open the pricing category drop-down inside the package and choose an option such as **Adult**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Enter the regular price" title-type="p">
    This is the standard price customers pay when there is no discount.

    Add the regular price for that category. For example, if adults pay 500 dollars for a trek, enter that as the regular price.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose whether the price is per person or per group" title-type="p">
    This setting changes how the price is charged at booking.

    Choose **Per Person** if each traveler pays the listed amount. Most tour operators use this option. Choose **Group** if the listed amount covers the whole booking, such as a private jeep tour for up to 4 guests.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn on the sale price if you're running a discount" title-type="p">
    Use this when you want to show a reduced price for a promotion or slow season.

    Turn on the sale toggle, then enter the sale price. For example, if your trek usually costs 500 dollars and you are offering a shoulder-season promotion at 400 dollars, enter 400 as the sale price. Travelers will see the original price crossed out.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the minimum participants per booking" title-type="p">
    This helps you avoid bookings that are too small for the trip to run.

    Enter the minimum number of participants required for that booking. For example, if you need at least 2 people for a guided canyon walk, set the minimum to 2.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Choose your booking dates

By default, a trip can be booked any day of the year. This works well for day tours, private departures, or trips you run on demand.

If you run trips on fixed departure dates, you can limit bookings to those dates instead. This is useful for guided treks, scheduled group departures, or seasonal tours that only start on certain days. The Trip Fixed Starting Dates extension gives you that control.

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
  Get the [Trip Fixed Starting Dates extension](https://wptravelengine.com/plugins/trip-fixed-starting-dates/?utm_source=free_plugin\&utm_medium=pro_addon\&utm_campaign=upgrade_to_pro) to set exact departure dates for a trip.
</Callout>

If you have already set the trip duration in hours on the **General** tab, you can also let customers choose a specific start time. This is helpful for day tours, rafting trips, city walks, or any activity with more than one daily time slot.

Once dates are sorted, you can finish by polishing how the package appears to customers.

## Set a description and choose your primary package

A short description helps customers compare options. Marking one package as primary makes sure the main option and the main price show first on the trip page.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a description for the package" title-type="p">
    A short summary helps travelers understand what is included.

    Add a brief description for the package. For example, you might write **Includes standard accommodation and all meals** or **Includes private guide and airport pickup**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Mark a package as primary" title-type="p">
    This tells the trip page which package to feature first.

    Click the mark icon next to the package you want to highlight. If you only have one package, that package is already the main one.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose a primary pricing category within the package" title-type="p">
    This controls which price customers notice first inside that package.

    Select the **Primary Pricing Category** for the package. For many operators, that will be the **Adult** price.
  </Step>
</Steps>
