The Trip Archive page is the page where visitors browse all your published trips. It helps people look through your tours, compare options, and find the trip that fits their dates, budget, and interests.

Customizing this page helps you show trips in a way that matches how you sell. For example, a trekking company may want to highlight difficulty and duration, while a day tour business may want travelers to see price, next departure, and destination first.

## Access the Trip Archive settings

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  You will need access to your WordPress dashboard and permission to change WP Travel Engine settings.
</Callout>

Before you change anything, it helps to know where these settings live. WordPress shows menu items in the left sidebar of your dashboard, and WP Travel Engine keeps the archive settings inside its main settings area.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open WP Travel Engine settings" title-type="h3">
    Log in to your WordPress dashboard.

    In the left sidebar, click **WP Travel Engine**. Then click **Settings**.

    You should now see the main settings screen for the plugin.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Go to the display settings" title-type="h3">
    At the top of the settings screen, click **Display**.

    This section controls how your trip pages look to visitors.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open the Trip Archive tab" title-type="h3">
    Click **Trip Archive**.

    Most of the settings on this page are here. A few card-related settings are under **Trip Card Display** on the same screen.

    After you make any change, click **Save**. That makes sure your updates appear on the front end of your website.

    <Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-fd8ba769-7008-47cf-98f1-e0d78d98350b/doc-08c376b3-8054-4dc6-b874-0c65a04a623e/1787560817487-hqwa193viwu-how-to-customize-the-trip-search-archive-page-archive-title.how-to-customize-the-trip-search-archive-page-archive-title?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" width="1024" height="504" alt="" />
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Good to know: Settings like **Show Slider**, **Show Wishlist**, **Show Map**, **Show Excerpt**, **Show Difficulty**, **Show Tag**, **Show Next Departure Dates**, **Show Available Months**, and **Show Trip Duration** are under **Trip Card Display**, not **Trip Archive**.
</Callout>

Now that you know where the settings are, you can start adjusting how travelers see your trips.

## Show the archive title

The archive title tells visitors what kind of trip list they are looking at. This is helpful when someone lands on a destination page or a trip type page from search results.

Turn on **Show Archive Title** to display titles such as **Destination**, **Trip Types**, or **Activities** at the top of the archive page.

Then choose the **Title Type** that fits your page:

- **Default** - Shows the standard page title.
- **Custom** - Lets you enter your own archive title.

A custom title can help you speak more clearly to travelers. For example, instead of a generic title, you might use “Everest Region Treks” or “Family-Friendly Day Tours.”

If you want visitors to understand the page right away, this is a good setting to start with.

## Change the trip view mode

The view mode changes how the trip list is laid out on the page. This affects how much information visitors can see at a glance.

Under **Trip View Mode**, choose one of these options:

- **List View**
- **Grid View**

**List View** works well when you want to show more trip details in each result. This is useful for longer tours where travelers compare duration, difficulty, and departure dates.

**Grid View** works well when you want a more visual layout. This is often a good fit for day tours, short getaways, or destination-based browsing.

If you are not sure which one to use, think about how your customers usually compare trips.

## Change the sort order

Sorting controls which trips appear first. This matters because many visitors only look at the first few results.

Under **Sort Trips By**, choose one of these options:

- **Recently Added** - Shows the newest trips first.
- **Top Rated** - Shows trips with the highest visitor ratings first.
- **Lowest Price First** - Shows the least expensive trips first.
- **Highest Price First** - Shows the most expensive trips first.
- **Shortest Duration First** - Shows the shortest trips first.
- **Longest Duration First** - Shows the longest trips first.
- **Alphabetical A-Z**
- **Alphabetical Z-A**

Pick the order that matches how people usually shop for your tours. For example, budget-conscious travelers may respond better to **Lowest Price First**, while a business focused on premium guided expeditions may prefer **Top Rated** or **Highest Price First**.

Once the sort order feels right, you can decide whether featured trips should stay at the top.

## Show featured trips on top

This setting keeps your featured trips at the beginning of the archive page. It works even if you use a different sort order for the rest of the trips.

Turn on **Show featured trips always on top** to pin featured trips first.

This is useful when you want to push best-selling tours, seasonal offers, or high-margin packages. For example, you might keep your Annapurna Base Camp trek at the top during peak booking season even if the page is sorted by price.

If you use featured trips, the next setting lets you control how many show up first.

## Change the number of featured trips

This setting controls how many featured trips appear on the archive page. It gives you more control over how much space those trips take up.

Adjust **Number of Featured Trips** to choose how many featured trips to display.

A smaller number keeps the page balanced. A larger number gives more exposure to promoted tours.

For example, if you run five main trips but only want to spotlight two flagship packages, set the number to match that plan.

## Enable or disable the side filter

Filters help visitors narrow down choices without leaving the page. This is especially useful if you offer many trips across different destinations or trip types.

Turn **Show Sidebar Filters** on to display the filter area on the archive page. Turn it off if you want a cleaner layout with fewer choices on screen.

A filter sidebar is helpful when travelers need to sort through a large catalog. For example, someone looking for a short spring trek may want to filter by destination, activity, or price range.

If your business offers only a few tours, hiding the sidebar can make the page feel less crowded.

## Add pagination or a Load More button

This setting controls how visitors move through more trips after the first set of results. It changes how browsing feels on the archive page.

Under **Display**, choose one of these options:

- **Pagination** - Splits trips across numbered pages.
- **Load More** - Adds a button that loads more trips on the same page.

**Pagination** is familiar and works well for larger tour catalogs. **Load More** keeps visitors on the same page, which can feel smoother on mobile devices.

For example, if you run many multi-day tours across several regions, pagination can keep browsing organized. If you have a shorter list of day tours, **Load More** may feel more natural.

The next group of settings changes what each trip card shows.

## Enable the slider

The slider changes the way trips are presented on the archive page. You will find this under the **Trip Card Display** tab.

Turn **Show Slider** on or off:

- **Enabled** - Trips appear in a scrolling slider.
- **Disabled** - Trips appear in the standard layout.

A slider can work well when you want to draw attention to selected trips. A standard layout is often better when visitors need to compare several tours at once.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Good to know: Sliders are more visual, but they can hide some trips until visitors scroll through them. If easy comparison matters more than promotion, the standard layout is often the better choice.
</Callout>

Once the layout looks right, you can fine-tune the details shown on each trip card.

## Enable or disable the feature tag

The feature tag helps travelers spot promoted trips quickly. It appears directly on the trip card.

Turn **Show Feature Tag** on to display the tag. Turn it off to keep the card design cleaner.

This is useful if you want people to notice tours you are actively promoting. For example, you might tag a newly launched group departure or a seasonal festival package as featured.

## Enable the wishlist feature

A wishlist lets visitors save trips they are interested in and come back later. This setting is under the **Trip Card Display** tab.

Turn **Show Wishlist** on to let visitors save trips for later review.

This can help when people are comparing several options before booking. For example, a traveler choosing between a 3-day wildlife safari and a 7-day trekking package may want to save both before deciding.

If your customers often plan in advance, this setting can be especially useful.

## Show the map

The map helps visitors understand where the trip takes place. This setting is under the **Trip Card Display** tab.

Turn **Show Map** on to display the location of each travel destination on the trip card.

This is helpful when location is a major selling point. For example, a traveler comparing tours in Pokhara, Chitwan, and Mustang can quickly tell which region each trip belongs to.

If your trip titles already make the destination very clear, you can decide whether the extra map detail is worth the space.

## Show an excerpt

An excerpt adds a short summary to each trip card. This setting is under the **Trip Card Display** tab.

Turn **Show Excerpt** on to display a brief description of each trip.

This helps travelers understand the trip before they click through. For example, a short line like “5-day guided trek with teahouse stays and mountain views” gives a much clearer picture than the trip title alone.

If your trips need a little more explanation to stand out, excerpts can help.

## Show trip duration

Duration tells visitors how long the trip lasts. This setting is under the **Trip Card Display** tab.

Turn **Show Trip Duration** on to display the trip length on each card. Then choose the format that fits your business:

- **Days and Nights**
- **Days Only**
- **Nights Only**

The right format depends on how you sell your tours. A lodge-based package may make more sense as days and nights, while a single-day activity may only need days.

For example, a 10-day trek with 9 overnight stays may be clearer in **Days and Nights** format. A city walking tour may look cleaner in **Days Only**.
